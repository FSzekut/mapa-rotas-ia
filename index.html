A route map of AI and software engineering

This is not a skill tree, it is a graph. Stations are shared between lines, and several destinations are reachable by completely different journeys: you get to AI Engineer through data, through backend or through product, and each route leaves you carrying something different. Pick a destination to see which lines run there, or click a station to find out what it is and who passes through it.

Same destination, different routes

The clearest case is AI Engineer. Three lines end there, and none of them is more legitimate than the others. What changes is where you are strong and where you are going to struggle. Job postings usually describe only one of these routes, which makes it look as though a single path exists.

The same holds for ML Engineer, reachable through data science (you can model but cannot operate) or through infrastructure (you can operate but cannot model), and for Fullstack, which is either frontend that learned the server or backend that learned the interface. In every case the predictable gap sits on the opposite side from where you started.

How to read this map

Station
A technology, a practice or a fundamental. The circle grows as more lines pass through it, and the biggest ones are the interchanges, which pay for themselves on any route.
Interchange
A station served by three lines or more. This is what you learn once and reuse in several directions, which makes it the best investment while the destination is still undecided.
Line
A plausible journey to a role. The order suggests a reasonable learning sequence rather than an obligation, and you can board halfway.
Terminal
The rectangular plates on the right are market roles, not certifications. Several of them show up in job postings under different names.
Depth
The three bars in the panel show how long a station usually takes to reach a level you can use in production, not how hard the concept is.
What is missing here
Communication, reading other people's code, technical writing and domain understanding have no station because they cut across the whole map. They usually decide more than the stack does.
A neutral map of the field. It describes routes people actually take, without recommending one. The sequences are plausible learning orders, not rigid prerequisites.
